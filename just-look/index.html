Just Look

May 23, 2011 · All Posts, An End to War, In Dialogue, Quick Thoughts, The Search for Enlightenment, To The Student

Recently, a curious child asked a curious question during a talk.

Child: What are you doing?

Takuin: Speaking.

Child: No, I know that. I mean what do you do?

Takuin: I remind people to look.

Child: What does that mean?

Takuin: (pointing up) Do you see those clouds?

Child: Yes.

Takuin: Most adults cannot see them.

Child: How come?

Takuin: When they should be looking, they are instead thinking.

Child: ... (looking)

Takuin: I remind them to just look.

Thanks, Ju.

If you are clear, and if there is injustice and suffering, you will act. It is not very complicated, although we tend to make it so.

As long as you live, expressing yourself through a physical body in a physical world, there will always be a choice. Always an action. It is not good or bad, but it is the truth. Forget about what you might have been told that your physical self is an illusion or whatever it is gurus are fond of saying. As long as there is the physical, maneuvering itself through the physical, there will be choice, action, all of that.

Your main question, regarding the above, is how can you move so that peace is your wake? How might you leave the room cleaner than it was when you entered? Just remember, you can always improve the life of someone, somewhere.

But let's be clear; it is not choice or action based on preference. Not a thought driven, self-aggrandizing thing. It is like seeing a poisonous snake in the road. You see it and you jump away. You don't think of how awful it is that a dangerous snake is in the road and how sad your family will be if it bites you and then who might show up at your funeral once you are dead and how long it will take your lover to move on to someone new and how long it will take before no living soul has any memory of you whatsoever. You just move. You act.

"Does this form of action arise from compassion?"

If it is as you've described, then that action IS compassion. Not that one arises from the other, although it may appear that way from the outside.

We just do our best. It is not because we want to get something out of it, or we want to be applauded or recognized, rewarded, or otherwise thought of as compassionate people.
